The Lucid Coding Manifesto

Vibe coding is a feeling. Lucid Coding is knowing.

Agentic engineering is how a lot of software is built now: agents write the code, and people direct them. Lucid Coding is a way to practise it with your eyes open. You know your system, your agents' thinking, and how each piece was built and judged.

Asif Raja

  1. Know your system.

    If you cannot say what your agents changed, why, and what proved it, you don't know your system yet. Every other principle serves this one.

  2. Agree the bar before the build.

    Write down what done means, as behaviours someone else can check, before anyone starts. A bar written after the work describes the work; it cannot judge it.

  3. A blind critic, never the builder, grades the work.

    The one who made it is the worst judge of it. A separate critic, who never sees the builder's reasoning, checks the real result against the bar.

  4. Evidence or it didn't happen.

    A pass carries its proof: test output, a screenshot, a log, a link. A claim with nothing attached counts as a fail.

  5. Every run is visible.

    What the agent saw, what it built, and why it passed or failed stay on the record, while the run is live and after it ends. Anyone on the team can read it without asking the agent.

  6. Every run teaches the next.

    Repeated gaps become lessons for the next brief, and past runs set the next budgets. The process improves on the record, where you can check it.

  7. Get the work done; performance comes with experience.

    Finish the work to the bar first. Speed and cost improve with time, and the record of every run is where that experience comes from.
